# Writing a Scene

**Audience:** A GM composing narrative for Coffee Pub Scribe.

How to lay out a passage inside a blockquote so it arrives at the table looking like a scene rather
than a wall of text.

## The blockquote is the unit

Everything Scribe does works on one blockquote. Put a scene in a blockquote and it becomes one card,
one handout, one export. Two scenes want two blockquotes.

Plain paragraphs inside the block work perfectly well on their own. Everything below is optional --
add the pieces you want.

## A title

**A Heading 4 at the top of the block becomes the card's title**, in a bar across the top with a
theatre-masks icon.

Put it first. A Heading 4 further down the passage is treated as a section break instead, with the
heading's text as its label -- useful for a long passage in two movements.

## Images and their captions

Drop an image into the block and it appears on the card.

**A Heading 5 immediately above an image becomes that image's caption.** The caption sits beneath the
picture on the card, even though you write it above.

A Heading 5 that is not followed by an image is treated as a section break instead, so it still reads
as a heading rather than disappearing.

## A divider

**A horizontal rule becomes a divider** on the card -- a thin dotted line. Useful between a
description and the conversation that follows it.

## Dialogue

This is the piece worth learning, and the one that most changes how a scene reads.

**A Heading 6 becomes a line of dialogue.** How you mark the name at the start decides which kind:

- **Bold the name** and it is a line spoken aloud, with a speech-bubble icon.
- *Italicise the name* and it is a character's private thought, with a thought-bubble icon.

Everything after the name is what they said or thought. Write it however you like -- quotation marks
are conventional but not required.

A Heading 6 with no bolded or italicised name at the start becomes an unattributed quotation, with
no speaker and no icon.

## Putting it together

A passage laid out like this:

    Heading 4    The Drowned Chapel
    Paragraph    Water has taken the lower pews, and something has been at the altar cloth.
    Heading 5    The Altar
    Image        chapel-altar.webp
    Rule
    Heading 6    **Kar-ahn** "We should not have come here."
    Heading 6    *Cyrus* Something down here is still awake.

arrives as a card titled The Drowned Chapel, followed by the paragraph, the picture captioned The
Altar, a divider, one spoken line and one unspoken one.

Here a real passage is shown as written in the journal and as it arrives in chat. Note that the
Heading 5 sits above the picture in the journal and becomes the caption beneath it on the card.

## What carries across, and what does not

**Your formatting survives.** Bold, italics, lists, tables and links to other Foundry documents all
come through into the card as you wrote them. A link to an actor or an item stays a working link that
players can click.

**Names are shown, never obeyed.** If a passage title or a speaker's name happens to contain
formatting characters or something that looks like a Foundry reference, it is shown as the characters
you typed rather than acted on.

**Colour is not yours to set.** The card takes its colours from the theme chosen in the settings,
which comes from Blacksmith. There is no way to colour an individual passage.
